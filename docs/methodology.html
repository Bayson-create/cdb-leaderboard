What is scored

The bench does not score a detector. It scores what the vehicle did after a controlled loss of LiDAR information travelled through the complete driving stack — detection, validation, tracking, prediction, planning, control and the vehicle interface — in a closed loop where the world reacts to the vehicle. Every entry is one fixed software configuration (perception model, fusion mode, controller profile, stack build) driven through exactly the same matrix.

The three indices are degradation-robustness indices: how much of each endpoint the configuration keeps at S1–S3 relative to its own S0 reference. They are not absolute performance and are not comparable across different maps, routes or vehicle models. The thesis behind the bench reports a scenario-conditioned profile rather than a scalar; the CDB Index exists so that a leaderboard can be sorted, and the per-scenario heat table is always one click away.

Score definition

For every scenario q, metric m on axis a, and severity s ∈ {S1, S2, S3}:

b      = mean over the five S0 runs of m            (baseline)
d_s    = mean over the five Ss runs of m
w_s    = max(0, sign_m · (d_s − b))                  (worsening; sign = +1 when lower is better, −1 when higher is better)
r_qms  = 1 − clip(w_s / scale_m(b), 0, 1)            (retention, 0…1)
r_qm   = (r_S1 + r_S2 + 2·r_S3) / 4                  (S3 counts double)
Axis_a = 100 · mean over q and m∈a of r_qm
CDB    = (Safety + Comfort & handling + Operation) / 3
  • Improvement is not rewarded. A metric that gets better under degradation (for example a faster completion time in the cut-in task) retains 1.0, no more. The index measures loss, not gain.
  • Scale. Each metric has a registered normalising scale so that S0 values near zero cannot blow up the ratio: mostly the S0 value with a floor, 25 % of the S0 completion time, and exactly one event for collisions and blocked events (any new collision at a severity zeroes that severity's retention).
  • Undefined cells are skipped, not imputed. TTC metrics apply only where ttc_applicable is true (not on the actor-free curve); route completion is undefined for the controlled stop.
  • No pooling across configurations. Every entry is normalised to its own S0. Two entries with different S0 behaviour can both score 100 if neither loses anything under degradation — which is the question the bench asks.

Metric registry

Handling metrics (hard / severe braking, MRM) are folded into Comfort & handling, matching the thesis family definition (thesis §3.6.2). The v1.1 spec therefore scores Safety over four vehicle-outcome endpoints, Comfort & handling over nine dynamics/handling endpoints, and Operation over four task endpoints. Localisation and perception-count metrics are recorded per run and shown on the model page but do not enter the index, because they are internal stage signals rather than vehicle endpoints.

Uncertainty

Intervals are 95 % percentile bootstrap intervals of the whole scoring pipeline: accepted runs are resampled with replacement inside every scenario × severity cell (2 000 draws, seed 20260907) and the axis and CDB indices are recomputed each time. With n = 5 per cell the intervals are wide by design; an entry whose interval overlaps another's is not reliably ranked above it.

Boundaries

  • Results hold for the registered configuration only: this simulator, map, route, vehicle model, controller profile and intervention family. They are not a public-road safety rate.
  • A stage record locates where a change was first measured in the chain; it does not identify the component that caused the vehicle response. No component isolation or matched controller swap is part of the bench yet.
  • Severity labels are comparable only inside one intervention family. A future channel-, rate- or camera-degradation family will carry its own S0–S3 and its own index.
  • Zero collisions do not establish safety when braking, MRM activity, jerk or completion time change — which is why the three axes are reported separately and the absolute columns stay visible.

The severity-family boundary above is exactly why every other registered mechanism (channel, rate, perception delay, semantic target loss, distance thinning, ROI cutout) is reported on its own axis rather than folded into this index — see Mechanism Robustness for that four-axis (spatial / density / temporal / semantic) view, its breaking points, and a benchmark comparison table.
